What students told us

We asked: what have you been told your body should be?

Through a sticky-note booth outside McKeldin Library and an anonymous online survey, students told us what body rules they’ve heard, who benefits, and what UMD could change.

75+

students took part

49 online survey responses + 27+ booth visitors, 37 sticky notes

63%

didn’t know UMD’s Counseling Center offers body image support

73%

said the pressure is tied to their gender, not just weight

21 of 49

Nearly half change how they eat or exercise because of it

48%

of booth visitors said reading others’ answers made them feel less alone

The sticky note wall

Anonymous answers, in students’ own words.

  • “Should be thin but also not too thin because otherwise I’m considered sick, but also not too big because otherwise I’m not taking care of myself enough.”
  • “Women are supposed to be skinny & not too muscular & be more feminine”
  • “Men are supposed to be more muscular & big & strong & fit”
  • “My skin should be lighter to be considered attractive”
  • “Asians are supposed to be skinny & tiny”
  • “Men are expected to grow facial hair to look mature and masculine”
  • “White, skinny, tall”
  • “Thin, tall, inaccessible.”
  • “Beauty companies profit from making us feel insecure”
  • “Gym memberships and supplement companies that profit from men’s insecurities”
  • “More info at health center”

If any of these sound familiar: you’re not the only one. Here’s where to talk to someone.

Who benefits?

Who gains from these body rules?

35%

Answering alone (online), the top answer was “me / my health.”

42%

Answering together (at our booth), people most often named beauty companies and influencers.

When students answered on their own, most said the rules were for their own good. But at our public booth, where people could read each other’s notes, the answers shifted toward the companies and influencers who profit. Seeing other people’s answers helped students notice the pattern. Body rules often benefit someone selling something.

Our recommendations

What UMD’s page should include

UMD’s current body image page lists a therapy group for women only. It sends students to a national organization that no longer offers a phone helpline, and its men’s resource link now redirects to that same organization. Here’s what we think it needs:

  1. Support for all genders, clearly listedSo men, nonbinary, and trans students know it’s for them too.
  2. Working helplinesReplace closed helplines with ones that are active today.
  3. Resources on race and culture, not just weightSkin color, height, hair, and cultural expectations shape body image too.
  4. A body image workshop or group open to all gendersA space where any student can talk about this with others.
